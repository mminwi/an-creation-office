Electrical standards

UL standards address product safety. IEC publishes international electrical standards. EN identifies European standards; CENELEC handles electrotechnical standardization. IEC and EN are not military standards, although a military contract can call for them. Choose by product, use, and target market.

Source directory · www.shopulstandards.com

UL Standards & Engagement — Electrical standards

Find UL standards for the product: UL 508A for industrial control panels; UL 50 and UL 50E for electrical enclosures; UL 62368-1 for audio/video and information/communication technology equipment.

What to check: Confirm product scope, market, edition, and certification route. A compliant component or enclosure does not certify the whole product.

IEC — Electrical product standards

International standards: IEC 60204-1 for machinery electrical equipment; IEC 61010-1 for measurement, control and laboratory equipment; IEC 62368-1 for AV/ICT equipment; IEC 60335-1 for household appliances; IEC 60601-1 for medical electrical equipment.

What to check: Choose the relevant family, particular parts, amendments and national adoption. These are starting points, not a checklist that applies to every product.

IP enclosure ratings at a glance

Read the two digits separately: the first is solid-object and dust protection; the second is water protection. Here are the three examples people often confuse.

IP56, IP57 and IP67 compared
CodeSolids / dustWater
IP565: dust-protected6: powerful water jets
IP575: dust-protected7: temporary immersion
IP676: dust-tight7: temporary immersion

Dust-protected allows limited entry; dust-tight does not. Immersion and water-jet tests are different, so IP67 alone does not establish IP66 performance.
