Funding and investor networks

Match geography, sector, stage, traction and founder criteria before applying. Check fees and sharing terms. A directory or network listing is not a funding promise. Rewards crowdfunding is distinct from equity investment.

Industry directory · angelcapitalassociation.org

Angel Capital Association — Member directory

Find angel groups and investment platforms, then visit each group's own site to check geography, industry and application process.

What to check: ACA is a directory, not a source of direct funding. Check entrepreneur fees, eligibility and information-sharing terms before applying.

For funding, project roles · Source: Angel Capital Association

Government source · www.sbir.gov

SBIR / STTR — Federal research funding

Starting point for participating agencies and research-funding opportunities.

What to check: Check current program availability, the specific agency solicitation, eligibility, and deadline; do not assume an opportunity is open.

For funding role · Source: Publisher or directory operator

Government source · www.grants.gov

Grants.gov — Search federal funding opportunities

Search the official federal grant opportunity directory by eligibility and subject.

What to check: Confirm applicant type, purpose, dates and registrations in the actual notice. Not every grant accepts a for-profit startup. Ignore unsolicited messages claiming you have already won a grant.

For funding, project roles · Source: Government agency’s own website

Investor network · www.goldenseeds.com

Golden Seeds — Women-led company funding

An angel network to investigate for U.S.-domiciled, early-stage companies in technology, healthcare and consumer sectors with a woman in an operating C-suite role.

What to check: Review leadership/equity, traction and sector criteria. A non-refundable $50 application fee is listed; confirm the current fee and eligibility before submitting.

For funding, project roles · Source: Golden Seeds · Source details

Investor network · tcaventuregroup.com

TCA Venture Group — Funding applications

Explore seed and early growth funding across technology-related sectors in the U.S. and Canada, with a strong Southern California focus.

What to check: Choose the relevant chapter and check its location, industry and traction criteria. Fee terms differ: Orange County lists an administrative application fee despite general no-fee wording.

For funding, project roles · Source: TCA Venture Group

Investor network · www.bandangels.com

Band of Angels — Seed investment

A California-based angel network considering U.S.-registered seed technology and life-science companies, including hardware and other technical products.

What to check: Review technology, market and stage fit. Its FAQ allows U.S. companies beyond Silicon Valley and lists no submission or presentation fees; check current terms before applying.

For funding, project roles · Source: Band of Angels · Source details

Provider learning resource · www.kickstarter.com

Kickstarter — Creator Handbook

Plan a rewards-based crowdfunding campaign: tell the story, build rewards, promote it, communicate with backers and prepare fulfillment.

What to check: Review current project eligibility, platform fees and delivery responsibilities before choosing crowdfunding. Budget manufacturing, shipping and campaign costs.

For funding, marketing, project, prototype roles · Source: Kickstarter

Primary guidance · www.sba.gov

SBA — Lender Match preparation

Prepare a plan, funding purpose and evidence for a lender conversation.

What to check: A match is neither an application nor loan approval. Get the actual lender’s current document and repayment requirements.

For funding role · Source: Primary publisher; public guidance reviewed

Primary guidance · www.sec.gov

SEC — Ready to raise capital

Assess financial, ownership, team and investor-readiness questions.

What to check: Preparing a pitch is different from offering securities; establish the relevant legal route before solicitation.

For funding, legal roles · Source: Primary publisher; public guidance reviewed

Primary guidance · www.sec.gov

SEC — Early-stage investors

Understand early investor types and capital-raising considerations.

What to check: Calling a round friends and family does not itself establish a securities-law exemption.

For funding, legal roles · Source: Primary publisher; public guidance reviewed

Primary guidance · www.sba.gov

SBA — Grants and limited funding opportunities

Find the scope of SBA-related grant assistance and research-program starting points.

What to check: SBA does not provide general grants to start or expand an ordinary business. Verify a current open solicitation and eligibility.

For funding role · Source: Primary publisher; public guidance reviewed

Primary guidance · sequoiacap.com

Sequoia — Writing a business plan

An investor’s presentation outline for problem, solution, market, model and team.

What to check: An example of pitch structure, not a universal investment eligibility rule or funding promise.

For funding, marketing roles · Source: Primary publisher; public guidance reviewed

Government source · www.sec.gov

SEC — Regulation Crowdfunding

Rules for selling securities to the public through a registered crowdfunding platform; the page gives a maximum of $5 million in 12 months (amended 2020). Disclosure and filing duties apply. Free.

Edition: SEC page dated 2024-06-21

What to check: Found through a web search; the office did not open the page. Confirm the current limit.

For funding roles · Source: Government agency (via search)

Government source · www.nimh.nih.gov

NIH/NIMH — SBIR eligibility criteria

SBIR eligibility: for-profit small business of 500 or fewer employees including affiliates, majority owned by US citizens or permanent residents (investor ownership rules differ by agency), principal investigator employed by the company. Award sizes vary by agency solicitation.

Edition: Web page

What to check: Found through a web search; the office did not open the page. Read the current solicitation of the agency you apply to.

For funding roles · Source: Government agency (via search)

Government source · www.sba.gov

SBA — Microloans

Small loans through nonprofit intermediaries for starting or growing a small business, often with training attached. Free to read.

What to check: Programs, amounts and rules change; read the current page before relying on it.

For business roles · Source: Government agency’s own website

SBA — 7(a) loans

SBA’s main loan program, made by banks with an SBA guarantee: working capital, equipment, real estate. Free to read.

What to check: Programs, amounts and rules change; read the current page before relying on it.

For business roles · Source: Government agency’s own website

Government source · www.sba.gov

SBA — 504 loans

Long-term fixed-rate loans for major equipment and buildings, through Certified Development Companies. Free to read.

What to check: Programs, amounts and rules change; read the current page before relying on it.

For business roles · Source: Government agency’s own website

Government source · home.treasury.gov

Treasury — State Small Business Credit Initiative (SSBCI)

Federal money given to states for small business loan, guarantee and investment programs; each state runs its own (in Wisconsin, through WEDC). Free.

What to check: Programs, amounts and rules change; read the current page before relying on it.

For business roles · Source: Government agency’s own website

Government source · www.cdfifund.gov

CDFI Fund — Community Development Financial Institutions

Mission lenders (community loan funds, some credit unions and banks) that lend to small businesses banks often turn down. Free.

What to check: Programs, amounts and rules change; read the current page before relying on it.

For business roles · Source: Government agency’s own website

Source directory · www.kiva.org

Kiva US — crowdfunded 0% small business loans

Small 0%-interest loans to US small businesses, funded by many individual lenders; social underwriting instead of credit scores.

What to check: Check current loan limits and terms.

For business roles · Source: Publisher or directory operator

Government source · www.irs.gov

IRS — Research credit against payroll tax for small businesses

A young company with qualifying research (product and process development often qualifies) can apply up to $500,000 a year of its research credit against payroll taxes, even with no income tax to offset (Forms 6765 and 8974). Free.

What to check: An accountant decides what qualifies and files it; the election is made on the original return.

For business roles · Source: Government agency’s own website

What to check: Programs, amounts and rules change; read the current page before relying on it.

For business roles · Source: Government agency’s own website
