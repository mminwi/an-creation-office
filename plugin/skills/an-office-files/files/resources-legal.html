Patents and legal help

A good match depends on the assigned person’s active registration, relevant technical work, conflicts, communication, written scope and fees. A patent agent’s authority differs from an attorney’s business-law practice. Use USPTO and state licensing records. Named firms are candidates to interview, not a quality ranking.

Government source · www.uspto.gov

USPTO — Patent basics

Official patent background and links to the application process.

What to check: Use current official information for the relevant patent question; keep unresolved issues for the legal role’s professional-review packet.

For legal, design roles · Source: Publisher or directory operator

Government source · www.uspto.gov

USPTO — Find a patent practitioner

Official route to the register of active patent attorneys and agents.

What to check: Confirm the assigned practitioner’s active USPTO registration. For an attorney, also check state-bar standing. Ask about technical experience, conflicts, who does the work, and a written scope and fee estimate.

For legal role · Source: Publisher or directory operator

Government source · www.uspto.gov

USPTO — Patent Pro Bono Program

Find regional programs that match eligible inventors and small businesses with volunteer patent professionals.

What to check: Check your regional program’s income, invention, and training requirements, availability, and any costs you must pay.

For legal, project roles · Source: USPTO — official program directory

Prepare disclosure and filing-timing questions for a patent practitioner.

Grace periods and absolute novelty in Europe (EPO and practitioner notes)

Europe requires absolute novelty: a public disclosure before filing, even by the inventor, can end patent rights; the US has a one-year grace period for the inventor's own disclosure (35 USC 102(b)(1)(A), MPEP 2153.01). Also https://www.epo.org/en/news-events/news/epo-consults-users-grace-periods-patents and https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2153.html .

Edition: Articles and MPEP section

What to check: Found through a web search; the office did not open the pages. Legal questions go to a patent attorney.
