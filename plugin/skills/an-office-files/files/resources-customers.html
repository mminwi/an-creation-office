Marketing and customer research

The marketing role starts with customer evidence, then chooses the message and channel. These sources help with research, practical skills and checking claims before publication.

Government source · www.sba.gov

SBA — Business planning and market research

Start with the market-research section: define customers, compare competing solutions and test demand, pricing and market size.

What to check: Combine published data with interviews or other direct research. Record assumptions and sources before using market estimates in a funding plan.

For marketing, design, project, funding roles · Source: U.S. Small Business Administration

Government source · www.census.gov

Census Business Builder

Explore U.S. customer demographics and business activity by location and industry; download maps and reports for market research.

What to check: Record the data year, geography and industry definition. Published statistics support research; confirm demand by speaking with prospective customers.

For marketing, design, funding roles · Source: U.S. Census Bureau

Commercial tool · trends.google.com

Google Trends

Compare search interest in product terms across time and places to explore terminology, seasonality and possible customer questions.

What to check: Scores show relative search interest, not sales, market size or an absolute count of customers. Record region, dates and search terms.

For marketing, design roles · Source: Google · Source details

Provider learning resource · developers.google.com

Google — Search Engine Optimization Starter Guide

Learn how to make a website easier for people and search engines to understand and discover.

What to check: Use the guide to improve useful content and site structure. Following it does not guarantee indexing or a particular search position.

For marketing role · Source: Google Search Central

Provider learning resource · academy.hubspot.com

HubSpot Academy — Digital marketing course

An introductory course covering marketing strategy, content, search, email, social media and advertising.

What to check: The listed course is free and uses a HubSpot Academy account. Distinguish educational material from optional paid HubSpot products.

For marketing, project roles · Source: HubSpot Academy

Government source · www.ftc.gov

FTC — Advertising and marketing guidance

Check product claims, endorsements, reviews, influencers, Made in USA statements and other advertising topics before publishing.

What to check: Choose the guidance matching the product and channel. Keep evidence for claims and review relevant disclosure rules with the responsible specialist.

For marketing, legal roles · Source: Federal Trade Commission

Government source · www.ftc.gov

FTC — Commercial email (CAN-SPAM) guide

Use the official checklist when planning promotional email, including business-to-business campaigns.

What to check: Review sender information, subject lines, postal-address and unsubscribe requirements. Also check applicable state, international and platform rules.

For marketing, legal roles · Source: Federal Trade Commission

What to check: The fact sheet governs; nothing outside it is stated as fact.

Model selling-plan, referral and fulfillment costs for the actual product.
