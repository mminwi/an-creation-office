SOP: Getting prototype boards made from a free-tools design. What to send the shop, handing a KiCad design to a shop that uses Altium, and bringing the boards up. Revision 1 — October 13, 2026.
Index: https://ancreationoffice.com/sop.txt
Lines are marked "office" or "published" (with the source to check).

ASK THE SHOP FIRST (office)
Before the layout is finished, ask the prototype shop:
- Which files do they build from (Gerber RS-274X or X2 with Excellon drill files, IPC-2581, ODB++), and in what form do they want the parts list and the placement file?
- Their design rules: minimum trace and space, minimum drill and annular ring, the layer stack-ups they offer, copper weights, board thickness. Set them in KiCad before routing.
- Do they assemble too? Do they buy the parts, or do you send them (consigned)? Lead times.
- Do they run a design-for-manufacturing (DFM) check, and will they send the questions back before building?
- Do they need the design itself, in their CAD (see below), or only the manufacturing files?

A SHOP THAT USES ALTIUM (office; published: Altium's KiCad importer documentation)
- Most shops build from manufacturing files, not from the design: Gerbers (or IPC-2581), drill files, the parts list and the placement file are the same whatever CAD drew them. A shop that designs in Altium can usually build a KiCad board without ever opening it in Altium. Ask them.
- If they do want it in Altium (to edit it, or for their records): Altium Designer has a KiCad importer (File, Import Wizard, KiCad design files) that turns a KiCad project into an Altium project. Check first which KiCad version their importer supports; KiCad can't save in older formats, so if their importer is older than your KiCad, they need to update it, or you send manufacturing files only.
- Imports aren't perfect (copper pours, design rules, some footprint details). Ask them to run their rule checks after importing and to compare against your Gerbers.
- Decide which copy is the master. The simplest rule: your KiCad project stays the master; any change the shop wants comes back to you to make in KiCad, and a new revision goes out. If the shop takes the design over in Altium, record that, and from then on changes go through them.

THE PACKAGE TO SEND (office)
One zip file named with the board name and revision, containing:
- Gerbers: every copper layer, solder mask, paste, silkscreen and the board outline (Edge.Cuts), and the drill files (plated and non-plated). Optionally IPC-2581 as well.
- Parts list (BOM): designator, quantity, maker, maker's part number, description, and which parts are not fitted (DNP).
- Placement file (pick and place, centroid) for assembly; ask the shop if their rotation convention differs from KiCad's.
- Assembly drawing as PDF: top and bottom, with designators and polarity marks.
- Schematic as PDF.
- Fabrication notes: layers and stack-up, thickness (commonly 1.6 mm), copper weight (2 oz on outer layers helps high-current boards), finish (ENIG is a good default for fine-pitch parts), mask and silkscreen colors, IPC class (class 2 is normal).
- The board's STEP model.
- A short readme: board name, revision, what changed since the last revision.
Before sending, open the Gerbers in a viewer (KiCad's GerbView or the shop's online viewer) and look at every layer. What you see there is what gets made.

HOW MANY (office)
For a three-board project: order five bare boards (often the minimum, and spares are cheap), assemble three, and keep the spares for rework or a fourth try. Order extra of the small parts.

BRINGING THE BOARDS UP (office)
1. Look: solder bridges, missing or rotated parts, polarity.
2. Meter: resistance between each power rail and ground (no short) before applying power.
3. Bench supply with the current limit set low: power up, check each rail, check nothing gets hot (a finger or an infrared thermometer).
4. One block at a time, using the jumpers or zero-ohm resistors to bring blocks in.
5. Then the functional tests of the project (for a charger: https://ancreationoffice.com/sop-charger-circuit.txt TESTING IT SAFELY).
6. Record each board's results by serial number in the job folder. Fixes on the board (bodge wires, changed values) go in a change list for the next revision.

REVISIONS (office)
Revision letter on the silkscreen. Each revision: a new zip, a short list of what changed and why, and the old files kept. Never change a released revision in place.
