SOP: Pictures for Mike's posts. Writing prompts for Grok Imagine (or another image AI), the house style, sizes, and the honesty rules. Revision 1 — October 13, 2026.
Index: https://ancreationoffice.com/sop.txt
Lines are marked "office" or "published" (with the source to check).

WHICH PICTURE (office)
1. A real photo is best: Mike at the bench, a prototype, a part, a screen with an office conversation. Nothing confidential, no employer's or customer's parts, logos or plant.
2. A press-kit card or the logo: https://ancreationoffice.com/press-kit/images/ (the cards are made with exact text; use them when words must be right).
3. An AI image: for an idea or a mood that has no real photo. Describe it with a prompt Mike pastes into Grok Imagine.

THE HOUSE STYLE (office)
The websites use realistic black-and-white images made with Grok Imagine. Keep posts consistent with them: realistic, black and white or muted color, natural light, real-looking shops, benches, tools and machines, people seen at work (not posing), no sci-fi glow, no robots shaking hands, no floating holograms, no stock-photo grins.

WRITING THE PROMPT (published: Grok Imagine guides, third-party; office)
- Write it like a brief to a photographer, in full sentences, about 30 to 80 words.
- Order: the subject, what's happening, the setting, the light, the style, the camera (for example: "shot on a 35 mm lens, shallow depth of field, eye level").
- Say what you want, not what you don't: Grok Imagine handles "no ..." poorly.
- Concrete details beat adjectives: "a gray injection molding press with its safety gate open, a technician in safety glasses checking a molded part under a work light".
- Ask for the shape you need: 16:9 landscape for X and LinkedIn link-style images, 4:5 portrait or 1:1 square for the LinkedIn feed.
- Expect 2 or 3 tries; change one thing at a time.
- Text inside AI images often comes out garbled: leave words out of the image and put them in the post, or put the image on a card made with real text.
Example prompt: "Black-and-white documentary photograph of an engineer's workbench at night: a laptop showing a chat conversation, a printed part drawing with handwritten notes, calipers and a small plastic prototype under a desk lamp. Realistic, natural shadows, 35 mm lens, slightly above eye level, 16:9."

SIZES (office; check each platform's current guidance)
LinkedIn: 1200 x 627 (landscape) or 1080 x 1350 (portrait, takes more of the feed); document posts as PDF. X: 1600 x 900 (16:9) or square. Keep important parts away from the edges.

HONESTY RULES (office; published: LinkedIn Content Credentials help)
- Never pass an AI image off as a real photograph of Mike's work, a real plant, a real product, a test result or a real event. If it could be mistaken for one, say it's an illustration in the post or the alt text.
- Never make images of real, identifiable people (other than Mike, with his OK), real company logos, competitors' products, or fake screenshots.
- LinkedIn shows a "CR" Content Credentials label on images that carry AI-generation data; that's fine and expected.
- Add short alt text describing each image (it helps people using screen readers and search).
