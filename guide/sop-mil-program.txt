THE QUALITY SYSTEM: AS9100 (published: SAE/IAQG AS9100D; a limited revision is being published as IA9100, check iaqg.org for its status)
AS9100 is ISO 9001 plus aviation, space and defense requirements. The company's procedures implement it; the office follows the company's procedures and knows what the standard asks so it can ask the right questions. The parts a project manager touches most:
- Project management and operational risk management: risks identified, owned, mitigated and reviewed through the project.
- Configuration management: every item has an identity and revision; changes are controlled and approved before they take effect; what was built and tested is traceable to the revision it was built from. (Guidance: EIA-649, MIL-HDBK-61.)
- Product safety and prevention of counterfeit parts: electronic parts from authorized sources, with traceability (AS5553 for electronic parts, AS6174 for materiel; DFARS 252.246-7007/7008 when flowed down).
- Design and development: inputs, reviews, verification and validation, and design changes, each recorded.
- Control of suppliers: requirements flowed down in purchase orders (quality clauses, special processes, source inspection, counterfeit controls).
- Production process verification: first article inspection to AS9102 for new parts and after changes.
- Nonconforming product: identified, segregated, dispositioned (use-as-is and repair need the right authority), and corrected.
- Aerospace APQP/PPAP: AS9145 is the AS9100-world equivalent of automotive APQP/PPAP; the customer decides whether it applies.

THE PROGRAM SIDE (office)
- Read the contract or statement of work first, with the owner: the deliverables (hardware, data items or CDRLs), the specifications and their exact revisions, the tests and who witnesses them, reviews (design reviews, test readiness review), schedule milestones, and quality clauses. Contracts often call out an older edition of a standard; the contract edition governs, not the newest one.
- Keep a compliance matrix: every requirement, how it will be verified (analysis, inspection, demonstration or test), the evidence, and status. Template: https://ancreationoffice.com/compliance-matrix-template.csv
- Keep a test matrix: each qualification test, the method and procedure, the article serial number and revision it runs on, the lab, the date window, and the result. Template: https://ancreationoffice.com/test-matrix.csv
- Plan the qualification sequence with the test lab: which tests share units, the order (usually the order the contract or the lab recommends), spares, fixtures, and the pre-test and post-test functional checks.
- Hold a test readiness check before each test: the unit's configuration matches the test plan, the procedure is approved, fixtures and cables are ready, the witness is scheduled.
- Track long-lead parts and single sources early; military connectors, cable and qualified parts can run long.

ALSO ON MOST DEFENSE HARDWARE (published; ask whether the contract invokes each)
- Item marking and unique identification: MIL-STD-130 (nameplate, part number, serial, and the 2D data-matrix UID mark when required). Decide the marking location and method early; it affects the enclosure and finish.
- System safety: MIL-STD-882 hazard analysis. A lithium-ion power device worn on a soldier has obvious hazards (heat, fire, short circuit); the customer may require a safety assessment.
- Reliability: a reliability prediction (MTBF), a failure reporting and corrective action system (FRACAS) through test and production, and part derating rules.
- Environmental stress screening (ESS): production screens (often thermal cycling and random vibration) that catch workmanship defects before shipment; not the same as qualification.
- Parts obsolescence (DMSMS): check the life-cycle status of key electronic parts and connectors before the design freezes; plan last-time buys or alternates (DoD SD-22 guidebook).
- Government inspection: DCMA or customer source inspection and witnessing of tests; plan who attends and when.
- Packaging and marking for shipment: MIL-STD-2073-1 and MIL-STD-129; lithium battery shipping rules if cells ship with the product.
- Human factors: MIL-STD-1472 (weight, size, sharp edges, use with gloves, cable routing, connector mating by feel, mounting on the vest such as MOLLE/PALS).

WHAT THE OFFICE PREPARES (office)
Schedules and milestone lists, the action list, meeting notes in plain words, the compliance and test matrices, supplier and quote comparisons, first article and nonconformance tracking, and short status reports for the owner. Record the technical detail the owner gives you. The company's quality system owns the approvals.

NEVER (office)
- Never mark something verified, passed or approved without the record that shows it.
- Never send, sign, order or release anything; prepare it and the owner does it.
