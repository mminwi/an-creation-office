Lines marked "Mike" are his; "published" lines cite the guide; "office" lines are the office's. Tier: deep.
Read this when parts show a defect, scrap rises, or a customer complains about appearance, dimensions or cracking.

1. START WITH THE STANDARD TROUBLESHOOTING GUIDES (Mike: the number one thing)
- The old defect guides are excellent and people don't use them: look up the defect ("burn here"), and the guide lists the likely causes and what to try. Seventy to eighty years of molding knowledge are in them. Use them first.
- Guides in the library (office searches, 2026-10-12; read through search results):
  - Resin supplier troubleshooters, for the resin you are running (example: BASF Injection Molding Troubleshooter, library: resin-troubleshooting-guides). Free.
  - Entec Polymers, Troubleshooting Guide for Injection Molding (by defect, cause and solution). Free download.
  - Douglas M. Bryce, Troubleshooting: A Guide for Injection Molders (IPLAS; defect by defect: black specks, blisters, burns, flash, flow lines, sink, splay, short shots, warp).
  - Randy Kerkstra and Steve Brammer, Injection Molding Advanced Troubleshooting Guide (Hanser, 2nd edition 2021).
  - Moldex3D defect definitions (short, with the mechanism of each defect).
- Published: change one remedy at a time; process variables interact (Husky PET troubleshooting guide).

2. THEN CHECK THE PLANT CAUSES THE GUIDES DON'T SEE (Mike, from the specialist procedures)
The guides list process remedies. Many defects Mike finds in plants start upstream, in the water, the dryers, the power or the press. Check these too:
| What you see | Look at | Procedure |
| Splay, nozzle spitting, process that won't settle | Wet resin: untested dryer, dead desiccant, reverse-phase outlet running the dryer fans backwards, resin over its moisture limit | sop-drying.txt |
| Burns, venting trouble | Filling too fast on high-speed (accumulator) presses: air can't get out | sop-scientific-molding.txt 2 |
| A color burning out at one spot | Shear heating at a choke point from filling too fast | sop-scientific-molding.txt 2 |
| Warp, uneven shrink, built-in stress | Big pressure drop across the part (slow fill), hot spots in the mold, ejecting above the set temperature, unbalanced cooling | sop-scientific-molding.txt 3, 7; sop-cooling-water.txt |
| One cavity short, cavities uneven | Hot drop heater with a cold tip and hot base; cavity or gate geometry; hot runner tolerances too loose on long runners | sop-scientific-molding.txt 4 |
| Cracking around the gate after a couple of years outdoors | Hold pressure released before the gate sealed | sop-scientific-molding.txt 5 |
| Slides, lifters or cores binding, galling, grinding in the ways; splay near them | No water in the moving parts: they heat, grow and bind | sop-cooling-water.txt 2 |
| Long cycle, high part cost | Cooling: unbalanced circuits, starved restrictive line, no water in moving parts | sop-cooling-water.txt |
| Fill time or shot weight wandering | Press not doing what it's told (speed pots dialed back, pressure), check ring not seating, process over 80% of press pressure, running at 100% injection speed with no control left | sop-scientific-molding.txt 1, 2, 3 |
| The process changes with the seasons | Resin temperature from outside silos (about 120 F in summer sun, 32 F in winter), humidity, weak dryers | sop-scientific-molding.txt 6; sop-drying.txt |
| Specks and visual defects in white, light or clear parts | Regrind in them (run zero there), or dust from a grinder running backwards | sop-drying.txt 4, 7 |

3. FIX, CONFIRM, RECORD (office)
- One change at a time, against the process sheet; record what changed and what the parts did.
- When the cause is upstream (water, dryer, power, press, heater), fix it there; a process tweak that hides it is a fix for the run, not the cure (Mike's words on hot drops, sop-scientific-molding.txt 4).
- Write the result back: the process sheet if a setpoint changed, the PFMEA if a new cause was found.

WHAT IT PRODUCES
A troubleshooting record per defect, in its own dated folder: the defect with photos, what the guide suggested, which plant causes were checked (table above), the change made, the result, and the cause found.

HANDS OFF
Resin maker's technical service (material-specific defects), tool shop and hot runner maker, press service, electrician (outlets), dryer service.
