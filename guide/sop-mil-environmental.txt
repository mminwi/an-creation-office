SOP: Military environmental testing (MIL-STD-810) for soldier-worn electronics. Revision 1.
Index: https://ancreationoffice.com/sop.txt
Lines are marked "office" or "published" (with the source to check).

THE STANDARD (published: MIL-STD-810H with Change 1 is current; confirm in ASSIST, and follow the edition the contract names)
MIL-STD-810 is not a pass/fail list. Part One says tailor the tests to the item's life: where it goes, how it is carried, stored, shipped and used. The tests are laboratory methods in Part Two; Part Three has climatic data.

TAILORING, IN ORDER (published, MIL-STD-810 Part One; office for the steps)
1. Life cycle environmental profile (LCEP): every phase of the item's life (factory, packaged shipping, storage, transport in vehicles and aircraft, worn on the soldier, in rain, in the cold, dropped) and what it sees in each.
2. Pick the methods and procedures that match those phases, and the levels (from the LCEP, measured data, or the standard's defaults).
3. Write the environmental test plan: methods, procedures, levels, durations, sequence, which unit runs which test, the functional check before, during (when required) and after, and the pass/fail criteria.
4. The contract or the customer's specification may already have done this tailoring; then follow it and record any gaps.

METHODS THAT USUALLY MATTER FOR A SMALL DEVICE WORN BY A SOLDIER (published, MIL-STD-810H Part Two; confirm which the contract invokes)
- Method 500 Low pressure (altitude): air transport, high ground.
- Method 501 High temperature and Method 502 Low temperature: storage and operation; check that batteries, connectors and seals work at both ends.
- Method 503 Temperature shock: moving between a heated shelter and the cold.
- Method 505 Solar radiation: heating and material degradation when worn in the sun.
- Method 506 Rain and Method 507 Humidity.
- Method 509 Salt fog: corrosion, especially at dissimilar metals and gasket flanges.
- Method 510 Sand and dust: seals, connectors and moving parts.
- Method 512 Immersion: when the item can go into water (river crossings); this drives the sealing design.
- Method 514 Vibration: transport and wear; ask the lab which profile (for example, the transport or the personnel-carried case) the contract calls for.
- Method 516 Shock, including the transit drop procedure: a body-worn item gets dropped.
- Others that sometimes apply: Method 508 Fungus (often handled by material selection and analysis), Method 511 Explosive atmosphere, Method 521 Icing/freezing rain, Method 504 Fluid contamination (fuels, cleaners, insect repellent).

WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE DESIGN (office)
- Immersion and rain set the sealing: the enclosure gasket, connector seals, and cable entries. Seal and EMI gasket often share the same flange, so design them together (sop-mil-enclosure.txt).
- Low temperature affects batteries (capacity and charging limits), elastomers (seal stiffness and compression set) and potting.
- Salt fog punishes dissimilar metals and bare gasket lands; finish selection matters (sop-mil-enclosure.txt).
- Drop and shock drive board mounting, connector retention, heavy parts and solder joints; workmanship class matters (sop-mil-electronics-build.txt).

RUNNING IT (office)
- Choose the lab early: accreditation (ISO/IEC 17025 with the methods in scope), capacity, fixtures, schedule, whether the customer wants to witness.
- Put the sequence in the test matrix: which units, which order, what functional check between tests. Keep spare units.
- A failure is data: record it, stop and agree the next step with the owner and the customer; don't retest silently.
- Keep only uncontrolled facts in the project repository (test name, unit serial, date, pass/fail, report number); the controlled report stays in the company's system (sop-mil-program.txt).
