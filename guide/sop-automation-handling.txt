SOP: Moving and processing small parts on an automation machine. Air (tube) feeding, nests and the index wheel, adhesive dispensing, framing and pneumatics. Revision 1.
Index: https://ancreationoffice.com/sop.txt
Lines are marked "office" or "published" (with the source to check).

AIR FEEDING THROUGH A TUBE (office)
- Small parts blown through a tube need: a tube bore sized to the part (enough clearance to move, not enough to turn or tumble when orientation matters), smooth bends with a large radius, smooth joints with no steps, and air pressure and flow tuned so parts arrive at a controlled speed.
- At the end of the tube: a decelerator or catch so the part doesn't bounce or get damaged, a sensor confirming arrival, and an escapement that releases one part at a time into the nest.
- Jams happen at joints, bends and the receiving end; make them easy to clear and detect them (a part that doesn't arrive in time is a fault).
- Static and dust: plastic parts in air flow charge up and stick; consider ionized air and keep the air clean and dry.
- Sending parts out: the same rules for the good and reject tubes; confirm each part left.

NESTS AND THE INDEX WHEEL (office)
- Each nest holds the part in a known position and orientation for the camera and the dispenser; the nest's tolerances plus the part's variation decide how much the vision and the dispenser must cope with.
- Clamping or vacuum holds the part during dispensing if it can move.
- Make nests replaceable and numbered; the PLC tracks parts by nest number.
- Check the nests for wear and contamination (glue drips) as part of maintenance.

ADHESIVE DISPENSING (published: dispensing equipment and adhesive makers' application guides; office)
- Methods: time-pressure (simple; dot size drifts with temperature, viscosity and level in the reservoir), positive displacement or auger (consistent volume), and non-contact jetting (fast, small dots, no touching the part). Small, repeatable dots at speed usually point to positive displacement or jetting.
- Adhesive choice is a product decision with process consequences: light-cure (UV or visible) adhesives cure in seconds under a lamp, which suits an inline machine; cyanoacrylates cure fast without light; others need time or heat. For a product that touches the body, the adhesive's biocompatibility is the product owner's decision (adhesive makers sell grades tested to ISO 10993).
- Cure: if light-cure, the lamp's intensity and exposure time at the station, the lamp's aging, and shielding operators from the light.
- Dot control: dot size and position checked by the vision system after dispensing; trend the size and alarm on drift.
- Keep it running: nozzle clogging (especially with fast-cure adhesives), purging at start-up and after stops, reservoir level, pot life and temperature, cleaning procedure.
- Ask the adhesive and dispensing makers for a sample run on real parts; they usually offer it.

FRAMING AND MACHINE BASE (office)
- Aluminum extrusion framing (80/20 and similar) is fast to build and change; use it for guarding, mounting plates and light structure. For the base under the wheel, dispenser and cameras, stiffness matters: vibration at the camera and dispenser shows up as bad images and misplaced dots. Use a solid plate or welded base where precision is needed, and level, anchored feet.
- Guarding with extrusion and clear panels, interlocked doors where the risk assessment calls for it (https://ancreationoffice.com/sop-machine-safety.txt).

PNEUMATICS (published: ISO 4414 pneumatic system safety; ISO 8573-1 compressed air quality classes)
- A filter-regulator with a lockable dump valve (energy isolation for lockout), a soft-start valve, and separate regulators where the tube feed and actuators need different pressures.
- Air quality: oil and water in the air contaminate the parts and the adhesive; specify filtration (and drying) for the feeding and any air that touches the product.
- Flow: tube feeding uses a lot of air; check the plant's supply.

QUESTIONS TO ASK (office)
How do parts arrive, at what rate, and in what orientation? What happens to a part that arrives wrong? Which adhesive, how is it cured, and who approved it for the product? What dot size and position tolerance, and how is it checked? How clean and dry is the plant air? How stiff is the base under the camera and dispenser?
