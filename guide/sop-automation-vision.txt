SOP: Machine vision for inspection and good/bad sorting. Revision 1.
Index: https://ancreationoffice.com/sop.txt
Lines are marked "office" or "published" (with the source to check).

START WITH THE DEFECT, NOT THE CAMERA (office)
- Collect real parts: good, every kind of bad, and borderline. Get the customer to sign off what counts as good and bad, with samples kept as the reference set ("golden" good parts and a labeled defect set).
- For each inspection, write what is measured or detected (presence of the adhesive dot, its position, size, coverage of the feature it must cover, part orientation) and the limit.

LIGHTING IS MOST OF IT (published: camera and lighting makers' application guides; office)
- The right light makes the feature high-contrast and the rest disappear: backlight for silhouettes and edges, low-angle (dark-field) light for raised features and texture, diffuse (dome or on-axis) light for shiny or curved parts, colored light and filters to separate a colored adhesive from the background. Some adhesives fluoresce under UV light, which can make a clear glue dot easy to see; ask the adhesive maker.
- Shield the station from plant light; strobe the light synchronized with the camera.
- Test lighting with real parts before choosing the camera.

CAMERA, LENS AND RESOLUTION (office)
- Field of view: the area to see plus margin for part position variation.
- Resolution: enough pixels across the smallest feature or tolerance; a common rule is several pixels across the smallest feature to detect it, more to measure it. Pixels needed = field of view / smallest feature x pixels per feature.
- Lens: focal length for the working distance and field of view; depth of field for part height variation (stop down, add light).
- Smart cameras (camera, processor and tools in one) suit simple stations; PC-based systems suit complex or multi-camera work. Use the plant's preferred brand when it has one.
- Trigger from position (encoder or PLC at the dwell), with an exposure short enough to freeze any motion.

THE DECISION AND THE REJECT (office)
- The camera sends pass/fail (and the reason) to the PLC, which tracks the part by its nest number until the divert point. Tracking errors (a decision applied to the wrong part) are a common failure; test them on purpose.
- Prove the reject: a sensor confirms a rejected part actually went down the reject path; if not, stop the machine.
- Fail safe: no image, a timeout or a camera fault rejects the part (or stops the machine), never passes it.
- Count rejects by reason; trend them.

PROVING IT WORKS (published: AIAG MSA manual, attribute agreement analysis; office)
- Run the labeled reference set through the machine many times in random order and measure false rejects (good called bad) and false accepts (bad called good). Agree the acceptable rates with the customer before the FAT.
- Re-run the challenge set after any change to lighting, lens, focus or vision settings, and at start-up of each shift if the customer requires it (challenge parts).
- Lock the vision settings and keep a backup; changes go through change control.

QUESTIONS TO ASK (office)
Who defines good and bad, with which sample parts? What false-reject and false-accept rates are acceptable? Does the adhesive show under visible or UV light? How much does the part's position and height vary in the nest? Is there plant light or vibration at the station? Who may change the vision settings?
