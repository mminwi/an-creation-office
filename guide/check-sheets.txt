PROJECT | New product development: the project review
Role: project
When: Start after hearing the idea; revisit before major spending, tooling, funding or launch.
Output: A concise project brief, assumptions, milestone plan and recorded review decision.
Save: product/concept/; links and agreed work in root plan.md


PROJECT-01 Who will use it, who pays, and what problem is worth solving?
Evidence to record: Name the user, buyer, task, alternatives and evidence from actual conversations. Separate founder beliefs from observations.

PROJECT-02 What are we making, and what is outside the first version?
Evidence to record: Concept revision, intended use, must-have outcomes, exclusions and measurable success criteria where known.

PROJECT-03 How many might we sell, at what price, through which channel and by when?
Evidence to record: Low/base/high units by month or quarter, geography and channel. Show prospects × purchase rate × units or stores × units per store per week × weeks. Record assumptions and capacity limits; market size is not a sales forecast.

PROJECT-04 What evidence says someone will buy it?
Evidence to record: Dated interviews, observed problems, price tests, pilots, orders or other evidence. Distinguish interest, nonbinding intent, paid orders and repeat sales.

PROJECT-05 What must be learned before the next investment?
Evidence to record: Open customer, IP, safety, technical, cost and channel risks; one next experiment, owner, finish condition and capped budget.

PROJECT-06 What are the milestones and dependencies?
Evidence to record: Concept review → feasibility and economics → prototype/user evidence → production readiness → launch → post-launch review. Tailor the sequence; do not imply every gate is already passed.

PROJECT-07 Who owns each result and decision?
Evidence to record: Named responsible people/assistants, exact inputs and revisions, agreed review participants and dates only when agreed.

PROJECT-08 Should we go, revise, hold or stop?
Evidence to record: Evidence reviewed, decision author/date, unresolved conditions, authorized next work and links to the exact released files. Revisit downstream sheets when an input changes.

Sources: nist-development, sba

ECONOMICS | Sales, price and the money left per unit
Role: funding
When: Before choosing a price, requesting production quotes or committing to a sales channel.
Output: A channel-by-channel low/base/high unit-economics model and cash forecast.
Save: business/finance/
The 25% rule is a starting target, not a universal law: a factory cost of $0.25 against $1.00 retail leaves room for other parties and expenses, but does not establish your profit. Validate the actual channel economics.
Illustrative assumptions only: retail $1.00; retailer buys at $0.50. Retailer gross margin is 50%, while markup on its cost is 100%. You receive $0.50. Factory cost $0.25 plus inbound freight/duty/packaging $0.05 gives $0.30 landed cost. Your gross profit before other selling costs is $0.20 (40% of your revenue). Another $0.10 of variable selling/returns costs leaves $0.10 contribution before fixed overhead, financing and tax. If a distributor buys from you at $0.40 instead, the same costs leave zero contribution. All numbers are examples, not quoted terms.
At $0.10 contribution, $10,000 of period fixed costs takes 100,000 sold units to break even under those assumptions. Show tooling recovery separately if it is not included. A tiny selling price is illustrative; minimum fees and fixed handling costs can dominate actual low-price products.

ECONOMICS-01 What will the customer pay, and what will we actually receive?
Evidence to record: Retail price excluding sales tax, discounts and refunds, and wholesale sell-in price. For marketplaces distinguish sales revenue from cash payouts after withheld fees; deduct those fees only once. Model direct, marketplace, retailer, distributor and licensing routes separately.

ECONOMICS-02 How many will we sell over a stated period?
Evidence to record: Units by channel and timing, evidence, confidence and sensitivity. Reconcile production orders, inventory and returns with sell-through; unsold stock is not revenue.

ECONOMICS-03 What does one acceptable unit cost at each realistic quantity?
Evidence to record: Dated quote versus estimate; materials, bought parts, assembly, packaging, inspection, scrap and yield. Keep one-time tooling separate; show any amortization assumption.

ECONOMICS-04 What does it cost to get that unit ready to sell?
Evidence to record: Inbound freight, duty/tariff where applicable, broker fees and receiving. Define landed cost and included items consistently; record currency, trade terms and quote date.

ECONOMICS-05 What other costs happen when we sell one?
Evidence to record: Channel fees, fulfillment/storage, outbound freight, sales commissions, promotion/advertising, payment fees, returns, warranty and chargebacks where applicable. Mark items already included to avoid double counting.

ECONOMICS-06 What margin and contribution remain?
Evidence to record: Gross margin = (our net sales − consistently defined cost of goods sold) / our net sales. Contribution = our net sales − all variable costs. Markup = (selling price − cost) / cost. Name whose margin each number describes.

ECONOMICS-07 How many units cover fixed costs, and when does cash run short?
Evidence to record: List period fixed costs such as rent, salaried/admin costs, insurance and fixed advertising/storage; split mixed costs into fixed and variable parts. Break-even units = period fixed costs / positive contribution per unit, rounded up; no finite break-even if contribution is zero or negative. Combined products/channels need an assumed sales mix. Track deposits, tooling, inventory, payment delays, taxes, debt service and reserve separately from accounting profit.

ECONOMICS-08 Which assumptions could make this unattractive?
Evidence to record: Test lower realized price/volume, higher freight/returns/advertising and slower collections. Record minimum acceptable contribution, peak cash need and the next quote or customer test.

Sources: sba, amazon-pricing

ERGONOMICS | People, use and ergonomic design
Role: design
When: While comparing concepts and again with representative prototypes.
Output: Use scenarios, measurable user needs, observed trials and a design-change list.
Save: product/concept/
NIOSH workplace and hand-tool guidance supplies screening questions. Adapt it to the intended users and task; hand-tool dimensions are not universal product limits.

ERGONOMICS-01 Who uses it, and who may struggle with it?
Evidence to record: Actual intended ages, abilities, hand/body sizes, left/right-handed use, strength, gloves and assistive needs. Do not design only for the founder or an average body.

ERGONOMICS-02 Where and how will people use, carry and store it?
Evidence to record: Lighting, wet/dirty use, temperature, clothing, workspace, reach, frequency, duration and one- versus two-handed tasks.

ERGONOMICS-03 Can they grip, lift, reach and operate it comfortably?
Evidence to record: Observe posture, reach, grip span, contact pressure, weight, balance, repetition and required force. Measure relevant dimensions and effort; do not invent a universal comfortable force.

ERGONOMICS-04 Can a first-time user understand controls and feedback?
Evidence to record: Setup, labels, visibility, tactile cues, mistakes, recovery and instructions; accessibility needs and actual observed misunderstandings.

ERGONOMICS-05 What foreseeable misuse or contact could hurt someone?
Evidence to record: Pinch, cut, burn, tip, choke, electrical and other applicable hazards, including children/bystanders. Feed design changes and safety requirements to the engineering role.

ERGONOMICS-06 Can it be cleaned, maintained and put away?
Evidence to record: Contact materials, residues, cleaning agents, disassembly, consumables, storage, service and disposal scenarios.

ERGONOMICS-07 What did representative users actually do?
Evidence to record: Prototype revision, participants and relevant characteristics, tasks, consent, observations, errors, discomfort, completion measures and limits. Small trials are learning evidence, not population-wide proof.

ERGONOMICS-08 Which design changes are required before the next prototype?
Evidence to record: Trace each requirement/change to an observation or stated need, owner and retest. Keep aesthetics, usability and engineering feasibility linked.

Sources: niosh-tools, cpsc-small-business, niosh-hand-tools

IP | What to protect, and why
Role: legal
When: Before public disclosure, a supplier disclosure, investor sharing or a protection decision.
Output: An invention disclosure, dated disclosure history and focused professional-review brief.
Save: company/intellectual-property/


IP-01 What business outcome do you want from protection?
Evidence to record: Exclude copying of a useful mechanism, protect appearance/brand, license, sell rights, attract investment, keep know-how secret or deliberately publish. Record markets, time horizon and budget.

IP-02 Exactly what do you believe is new?
Evidence to record: Explain the technical problem, mechanism/process, alternatives, differences, drawings and versions. Identify what may be functional, ornamental, branding or confidential know-how; do not turn a vague idea into a patentability conclusion.

IP-03 What related products and publications are known?
Evidence to record: Dated searches, terms, jurisdictions/classes, relevant documents/claims and unanswered questions. An initial search is not proof of novelty or completeness.

IP-04 Who contributed, owns rights or has contractual interests?
Evidence to record: Contributors and what they contributed; employment, assignments, contractor terms, university/sponsor interests and agreements. Inventorship, patent ownership, company shares and project access are different.

IP-05 What has already been disclosed or offered for sale?
Evidence to record: Exact dates, material/revision, audience, confidentiality terms, public demos, crowdfunding, online posts, offers and sales. Have relevant filing options assessed before further disclosure; do not assume a worldwide grace period.

IP-06 Which protection route fits the objective?
Evidence to record: Utility patent versus design patent, trademark or trade secret as applicable; costs, territories, timing, discoverability/reverse engineering and practical secrecy measures. A provisional is not an issued patent and does not cover design inventions.

IP-07 Could making or selling it infringe someone else’s rights?
Evidence to record: Separate patentability research from freedom-to-operate review of relevant claims, territory, product and dates. A patent grants an exclusion right, not permission to manufacture or sell.

IP-08 What is the decision, and what needs professional review?
Evidence to record: Exact questions, proposed protection/licensing approach, ownership gaps, disclosure urgency and dated decision. Record actual applications/receipts only if they exist; a draft or checklist is not a filing.

Sources: uspto-patent-overview, uspto-provisional, uspto-trade-secrets, trademark-basics, uspto-design

DFM | Design for manufacture and assembly
Role: engineering
When: Before prototype quotes, tooling release and any production design change.
Output: A reviewed DFM/DFA record, requirements and matched manufacturing package.
Save: product/engineering/; requirements in product/requirements/


DFM-01 What performance and safety requirements drive the design?
Evidence to record: Intended use, loads, life, environment, interfaces, applicable market rules and acceptance criteria with sources.

DFM-02 Does the process fit material, geometry and volume?
Evidence to record: Compare machining, molding, casting, sheet metal, additive or other processes against quantity, finish, lead time, tooling and total cost; identify process-specific risks.

DFM-03 Are features realistic for the chosen process?
Evidence to record: For molding: walls, draft, ribs/bosses, undercuts, shrinkage and ejection. For machining: tool access, deep pockets and corner radii. For sheet metal: bends, radii and hole spacing. Obtain the actual supplier’s limits; no universal wall/draft number.

DFM-04 Are tolerances functional and inspectable?
Evidence to record: Critical dimensions, datums, fits, tolerance stack-ups, finishes and inspection methods. Avoid needless tight tolerances; specify those required for function or safety.

DFM-05 Can the parts be assembled consistently?
Evidence to record: Reduce unnecessary parts/fasteners, use standard components, access tools, orient and locate parts, prevent wrong assembly, protect wires/seals and test service/disassembly.

DFM-06 Can material or supplier substitutions change performance?
Evidence to record: Material/grade, flame/contact/corrosion requirements, finishes, standard hardware and qualified equivalents. Preserve designer catalog references separately from purchasing sources.

DFM-07 Are the files complete and at one revision?
Evidence to record: Bill of materials, STEP solid and dimensioned drawings where applicable, materials/finishes, critical characteristics, assembly notes, quantity breaks and inspection/acceptance requirements. A render is not a release package.

DFM-08 What did the manufacturer flag, and who accepted changes?
Evidence to record: Dated supplier DFM feedback, disposition, affected requirements, cost/tooling impact, owner and verification/retest. Obtain review before releasing revised work.

Sources: protolabs-dfm, nist-development, cpsc-small-business, protolabs-walls

PROTOTYPE | What the prototype needs to prove
Role: prototype
When: Before each prototype build and before reporting a result.
Output: A test plan, build record and evidence-based test report.
Save: product/prototypes/ and product/testing/


PROTOTYPE-01 Which question is this prototype answering?
Evidence to record: Appearance, usability, fit, mechanism, strength, life, process or customer acceptance; list what it cannot demonstrate.

PROTOTYPE-02 What is the exact configuration?
Evidence to record: Model/drawing/BOM revision, materials, deviations from production, build method and sample identifiers.

PROTOTYPE-03 How will we judge success before testing?
Evidence to record: Requirement ID, method, numerical/observable acceptance criterion, environment, equipment, calibration where needed and planned sample size rationale.

PROTOTYPE-04 Is the test safe and representative?
Evidence to record: Foreseeable hazards, setup limits, operator competence, exposure safeguards, realistic users/conditions and specialist or lab involvement where needed.

PROTOTYPE-05 What happened, including failures?
Evidence to record: Raw measurements, units, photos, observations, anomalies and deviation from method. Separate measured from simulated and predicted performance.

PROTOTYPE-06 What can we conclude, and what remains unproven?
Evidence to record: Verification: meets specified requirements. Validation: serves intended users/use. State sample and prototype limits, owner of failures, required changes and retest before a release claim.

Sources: nasa-verification, cpsc-small-business, nasa-validation

PRODUCTION | Suppliers and production readiness
Role: manufacturing
When: Before comparing quotes, placing production orders or scaling.
Output: Comparable quotes, supplier evaluation and a production-readiness review.
Save: business/suppliers/ and product/manufacturing/


PRODUCTION-01 Are all suppliers quoting the same thing?
Evidence to record: Exact revision/material/finish/quantities, inspection, packaging and acceptance requirements; assumptions and exclusions alongside each quote.

PRODUCTION-02 Can the supplier actually make and inspect it?
Evidence to record: Relevant capabilities, process limits, capacity, sample evidence, quality system scope, references, subcontractors and production location. Directory membership alone is not qualification.

PRODUCTION-03 What are the real costs and commitments?
Evidence to record: Unit prices at realistic volumes, tooling/NRE, MOQ, lead times, payment/deposit terms, currency, freight/trade terms, duties, scrap, quote expiry and cancellation/change terms.

PRODUCTION-04 Who owns tooling, data and changes?
Evidence to record: Tool ownership, custody, maintenance and transfer access; drawings/IP/confidentiality, approved substitutions and who can authorize changes.

PRODUCTION-05 How will we accept incoming parts and production output?
Evidence to record: First-article/pilot evidence, critical characteristics, sampling rationale, measurement methods, traceability, nonconformance/rework and corrective actions.

PRODUCTION-06 Can we deliver and recover from interruption?
Evidence to record: Pilot quantity, ramp/capacity plan, long-lead parts, approved alternate sources, packaging/shipping trials, inventory and reorder points.

PRODUCTION-07 What must be in place after sale?
Evidence to record: Warranty, spare parts, service, complaints, traceability and recall/incident process appropriate to product/market.

PRODUCTION-08 Is the first production order ready?
Evidence to record: Close or explicitly disposition DFM/test gaps, confirm current file package and cash need with the funding role. Record the person’s release decision; never infer authorization to place an order.

Sources: nist-supplier, cpsc-small-business

FUNDING | Prepare for funding and finance conversations
Role: funding
When: When a defined milestone needs outside money; tailor to the actual lender, investor or program.
Output: A dated funding brief with linked evidence, financial model and recipient-specific document list.
Save: business/funding/; supporting numbers in business/finance/
SBA does not provide general grants to start or expand an ordinary business. A research program or other grant requires a currently open, eligible solicitation.
Friends-and-family funding is not automatically exempt from securities rules. The legal role helps identify the legal route before an investment offer is shared.

FUNDING-01 How much money is needed, for what milestone and by when?
Evidence to record: Use-of-funds line items, founder contribution, quotes/estimates, runway, contingency and measurable milestone. Separate startup spending, inventory and operating costs.

FUNDING-02 Which funding route fits the business?
Evidence to record: Self-funding/revenue, loan, equity, eligible grant, licensing or rewards crowdfunding. Compare repayment, dilution/control, eligibility, restrictions, timing and ability to deliver.

FUNDING-03 What evidence supports the commercial story?
Evidence to record: Problem, customer, product/demo, alternatives/competition, market assumptions, business model, channel economics, traction and team/gaps. Do not count interest as sales.

FUNDING-04 Are the financials coherent?
Evidence to record: Actual financial statements if available; forecast income, balance sheet and monthly cash flow; unit economics, break-even, low/base/high scenarios, working capital and debt service where relevant. Clearly label pre-revenue estimates.

FUNDING-05 What will a lender need?
Evidence to record: Recipient’s current checklist: business plan, purpose/amount, historical tax/financial records where applicable, forecasts, repayment source, ownership, relevant collateral/guarantee questions and contracts. Request sensitive records securely, not in public materials.

FUNDING-06 What will an investor need?
Evidence to record: Investor fit by stage/sector; concise pitch, funding ask and milestone, market/traction evidence, cap table, team, entity/IP ownership records, contracts and financial model. Explain how investors might receive a return; review dilution and control with advisers. Organize controlled diligence access and unresolved issues.

FUNDING-07 What will a grant or crowdfunding route need?
Evidence to record: For a grant: current open solicitation, fit, eligibility, registrations, work plan, deliverables, budget/allowable costs and deadlines. For rewards: prototype, production/fulfillment budget, delivery assumptions and rewards. Securities crowdfunding is a different regulated route.

FUNDING-08 Is this ready to share with this recipient?
Evidence to record: Current requested format/documents, consistent numbers and revisions, confidentiality/public-disclosure implications, unanswered questions, proposed next step and human authorization. No guarantee of funding or invented valuation/terms.

Sources: sba, sba-lender, sec-ready, grants-search, kickstarter-handbook, sec-early-investors, sba-grants, sequoia-plan

LAUNCH | Customer evidence, marketing and launch
Role: marketing
When: While testing demand and before a campaign or launch commitment.
Output: A channel-specific launch plan, evidence-backed messaging and readiness decision.
Save: business/marketing/


LAUNCH-01 Who is the first customer and why will they switch?
Evidence to record: Narrow segment, use case, alternatives, differentiated benefit and evidence from interviews/tests.

LAUNCH-02 Which offer and price have we tested?
Evidence to record: Actual experiment, audience, price, offer, budget, duration and success measure. Separate paid conversion and repeat use from views/likes or expressions of interest.

LAUNCH-03 What claims can we support?
Evidence to record: Claim-to-evidence list, demonstrations, endorsements/disclosures, product photos and instructions; the legal role reviews disclosure/IP concerns before publishing confidential details.

LAUNCH-04 Which channel fits customer and economics?
Evidence to record: Direct web sales, marketplace, retail wholesale, distributors, licensing or combinations; buyer behavior, net receipts, acquisition cost, fulfillment and cash implications.

LAUNCH-05 Are launch materials and operations ready?
Evidence to record: Product identifiers, packaging/dimensions, listing/sell sheet, images, substantiated copy, instructions, support, inventory, shipping/returns and applicable safety documentation.

LAUNCH-06 What does a small launch have to prove?
Evidence to record: Pilot quantity/budget, target conversion, contribution, returns, reviews/complaints, stock availability, stop/adjust criteria and a scheduled review owner.

LAUNCH-07 Who does what after launch?
Evidence to record: Customer support, returns/warranty, replenishment, marketing measurement, complaint escalation and decision to scale. Date the plan; confirm permissions before publishing.

Sources: ftc-advertising, sba, gs1-barcodes

RETAIL | Amazon, Walmart and Lowe’s: getting ready for buyers
Role: marketing
When: After choosing a realistic channel and before applying, pitching or accepting terms.
Output: A buyer-ready sell sheet, channel economics and route-specific application checklist.
Save: business/marketing/; costs in business/finance/


RETAIL-01 Which route are we seeking?
Evidence to record: Record marketplace seller, retailer wholesale supplier, private-brand supplier or distributor route. Marketplace approval does not mean store placement, and an application is not a purchase order.

RETAIL-02 Does the product fit this retailer’s customers and assortment?
Evidence to record: Comparable listings/shelf alternatives, differentiation, target retail price, evidence of demand, packaging and current assortment gap hypothesis.

RETAIL-03 Can we give a buyer a concise commercial packet?
Evidence to record: Sell sheet with use/benefit, photos/demo, dimensions, identifiers, target retail and wholesale prices, retailer margin assumption, MOQ/case pack, lead time, capacity, origin, test/compliance evidence and contact. Samples must match the stated revision.

RETAIL-04 Will each sale and payment schedule work for us?
Evidence to record: Get actual category fees, freight, fulfillment, allowances/promotions, broker commission, returns, damage, deductions/chargebacks, payment terms and inventory commitments. Model cash needed before payment; do not treat typical margins as an offer.

RETAIL-05 Can we meet operations and compliance requirements?
Evidence to record: Verify current insurance, product safety/testing/labeling, GTIN/UPC or exemptions, package/case data, EDI/ASN if required, fulfillment/service metrics, returns and recall readiness with the retailer. These are categories to confirm, not universal terms.

RETAIL-06 Would a representative or distributor help?
Evidence to record: A sales rep/broker typically helps sell for a fee or commission; a distributor may buy/resell and handle inventory. Verify category/account relationships and references. Negotiate territory, exclusivity, services, compensation trigger, expenses, stock/title risk, termination and conflicts. A claimed connection is not guaranteed shelf space.

RETAIL-07 What is the smallest credible entry?
Evidence to record: Direct application, limited online/pilot assortment, regional opportunity, appropriate supplier event or an established channel partner. Start with the retailer’s official route; a solo founder can apply but still must meet capability and qualification requirements.

RETAIL-08 What is still required before submission or commitment?
Evidence to record: Save current portal requirements/date, application status, buyer feedback, open terms and exact package revision. The person authorizes submissions and agreements. Never imply that a submitted profile, listing or verbal interest equals acceptance.

Amazon: third-party selling
Start with Seller Central registration, separate from wholesale Vendor Central. Prepare identity/business and bank verification, category/product approvals, identifiers or an allowed exemption, brand rights and listing materials. Choose merchant fulfillment or FBA using the actual packaged size/weight and current fee calculator. Recheck selling-plan, referral, fulfillment, storage and returns costs; Brand Registry is a separate eligible-brand program.
Sources: https://sell.amazon.com/sell/registration-guide; https://sell.amazon.com/pricing

Walmart: marketplace or retail supplier
Marketplace is third-party online selling. Published qualifications include business verification (SSN is not accepted as the business identifier), prior eCommerce success, suitable identifiers/catalog and WFS or U.S. warehouse fulfillment with returns capability. Confirm current exceptions and new-seller payment holds. Store/wholesale supply uses the separate supplier application, requirements and official checklist. Open Call is another possible route for eligible U.S.-made, grown or assembled products when an application window is open. None guarantees shelf space.
Sources: https://marketplace.walmart.com/about-walmart-marketplace/; https://marketplace.walmart.com/seller-registration-guide/; https://corporate.walmart.com/suppliers/apply-to-be-a-supplier; https://corporate.walmart.com/content/dam/corporate/documents/suppliers/apply-to-be-a-supplier/supplier-checklist.pdf; https://corporate.walmart.com/suppliers

Lowe’s: branded, private-brand or marketplace
The official supplier page directs branded retail proposals to RangeMe and private-brand sourcing to PROVIS. Lowe’s Marketplace is a separate curated third-party route; check home-improvement category fit, U.S. inventory/shipping and product-data requirements. Obtain actual commissions, payment/return terms and service rules during onboarding. Some vendor-only or forthcoming documents were unavailable in this public review; leave those terms unverified.
Sources: https://www.lowes.com/l/about/suppliers; https://www.lowes.com/l/about/lowes-marketplace; https://seller.lowes.com/mirakl-seller-documentation/

Sources: amazon-registration, amazon-pricing, walmart-suppliers, walmart-marketplace, lowes-suppliers, lowes-marketplace, gs1-barcodes, walmart-requirements, walmart-checklist, walmart-registration, walmart-open-call, lowes-seller-docs, trade-representatives

BEGIN ADVANCE NOW
Advance Now: help the inventor take a useful step today. Treat risk analysis as a list of opportunities to investigate, questions to ask and routes around or through a problem. Ordinary uncertainty, cost, imperfect ergonomics and unanswered checklist items do not automatically stop or slow the whole project. Keep analysis proportionate; do the useful authorized work available now. Look for a smaller experiment, an existing part, a different material or process, a revised design, staged help, a lower-cost route or someone with relevant experience. Keep a short roadblock list using existing issue IDs: concern and uncertainty, affected activity, possible workaround, question to ask, person who could help, owner, what advances now, revisit trigger and evidence/revision. Carry these questions into relevant conversations and handoffs. A later contact may reveal an easy solution or help carry the load; seek that opportunity without guaranteeing it. Do not turn every concern into a stop sign or end with a discouraging wall of risks. End with the next practical action. If one concrete activity needs a safety control, a disclosure decision, permission or a required review, identify that exact activity and keep progressing on other useful work or a safer route. Keep the evidence honest and preserve existing spending, sending, filing and publication authority. Do not insist on paid professional help before every preparation step: investigate available self-help and assistance routes. Do not promise that a later patent review can repair missing disclosure. Respect the person's choice to pause; never invent completion. Supervisors pass this same posture to temporary helpers.
