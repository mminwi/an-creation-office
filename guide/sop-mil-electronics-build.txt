SOP: Building military electronics. Workmanship classes, boards, conformal coating, cables and shielding. Revision 1.
Index: https://ancreationoffice.com/sop.txt
Lines are marked "office" or "published" (with the source to check; confirm current revisions with IPC and in the contract).

WORKMANSHIP CLASSES (published: IPC)
IPC standards have three classes. Class 3 is high-performance and harsh-environment equipment (military, aerospace, medical life support): the tightest acceptance criteria. Military soldier equipment is normally Class 3; the contract or drawing says.
- IPC-A-610: acceptability of electronic assemblies (what a good solder joint and assembly look like).
- J-STD-001: requirements for soldered assemblies (materials, processes, training). A space addendum exists for space hardware.
- IPC-6012: qualification and performance of rigid printed boards (Class 3 for this kind of product); the board fabricator builds to it.
- IPC-2221/IPC-2222: board design rules (spacing, current, via and land design).
- IPC/WHMA-A-620: cable and wire harness assemblies.
- IPC-7711/7721: rework and repair of assemblies.
- ANSI/ESD S20.20: ESD control program in the factory.
- J-STD-020 / J-STD-033: moisture sensitivity of parts (baking and handling before reflow).

CONFORMAL COATING (published: IPC-CC-830 is the qualification standard for coating materials and replaced MIL-I-46058C; IPC-HDBK-830 is the guide)
- Coating types: acrylic (AR), urethane (UR), silicone (SR), epoxy (ER), parylene (XY) and newer types. Choose by environment (humidity, salt fog, fluids), reworkability, temperature and the customer's requirement.
- Decide early which areas must not be coated: connectors, test points, gasket and bonding surfaces, adjustment points; mask them.
- Inspect coverage (most coatings fluoresce under UV) and thickness to the drawing.

CABLES AND SHIELDING (published: MIL-DTL-27500 shielded and jacketed cable, M22759 wire, AS85049 backshells; check ASSIST and SAE for current revisions)
- Shielded cables with the shield terminated 360 degrees in the connector backshell; overmolding or a sealed backshell for immersion.
- Strain relief that survives the drop and vibration tests and a soldier pulling on the cable.
- Military connectors and cable can be long-lead; order samples early.

POTTING AND MOUNTING (office)
Potting or staking heavy parts helps with shock and vibration but complicates rework and heat; decide with the electrical engineer and the build house. Board mounting points and connector retention must survive the drop test.

FIRST ARTICLE AND BUILD RECORDS (published: AS9102; office)
First article inspection on the first production build and after changes; serial-number traceability of the board, the assembly and key parts; build travelers. The records stay in the company's system; the office tracks status and numbers (sop-mil-program.txt).

QUESTIONS TO ASK THE BUILD HOUSE (office)
Are you certified to AS9100 and trained to J-STD-001 / IPC-A-610 Class 3? How do you control counterfeit parts and moisture-sensitive parts? Which coating and masking process, and how is coverage inspected? Can you build cables to IPC/WHMA-A-620 Class 3, or who does? What is the lead time on first articles?
